How to Paint Kitchen Cabinets Like a Pro: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

If your kitchen feels dated but a full renovation is not in the budget, painting your cabinets is the single most cost-effective upgrade you can make. A fresh coat of paint can transform a tired kitchen into something you are genuinely proud to show off — and you can do it yourself over a weekend.

We have painted three sets of cabinets in our own homes, and we learned what works and what does not the hard way. In this guide, we will walk you through every step, from choosing the right paint to handling those tricky corners around the sink. No shortcuts, no gimmicks — just honest advice that actually works.

What You Will Need

Before you start, gather these supplies. Skimping on materials is the fastest way to end up with a disappointing finish.

  • High-quality cabinet paint (alkyd or enamel-based — more on this below)
  • Primer designed for laminate or wood cabinets
  • Screwdriver or drill
  • Painter's tape (Frog Tape or equivalent)
  • Drop cloths
  • Sandpaper (120 and 220 grit)
  • Tack cloth
  • Painters pyramids or small wooden blocks
  • Foam roller kit (smooth finish rollers)
  • Angled brush for detail work
  • Degreaser or TSP substitute
  • Clean rags and sponges
  • Vacuum with brush attachment

Step 1: Remove the Doors and Hardware

Start by taking everything off. Remove all cabinet doors, drawers, and hardware. Label each door with a piece of tape on the back and note where it goes — this will save you a massive headache during reassembly. Keep screws in labeled zip bags.

Do not skip this step. Painting cabinets with the doors attached is tempting, but it leads to drips, uneven coverage, and a very messy finish.

Step 2: Clean Thoroughly

Kitchen cabinets accumulate years of grease and grime. Mix a degreaser or TSP substitute with warm water and scrub every surface — fronts, backs, frames. Pay extra attention to areas near the stove and sink. Rinse with clean water and let everything dry completely.

This step is non-negotiable. Paint will not stick to greasy surfaces. If you wipe down a seemingly clean door and your rag comes back yellow, keep scrubbing.

Step 3: Sand and Smooth

Lightly sand all surfaces with 120-grit sandpaper, then follow with 220-grit for a smooth finish. You are not trying to strip the old finish — just scuff it up so the primer has something to grip.

Wipe away dust with a tack cloth. Vacuum the surrounding area as well. Dust is your enemy here.

Step 4: Prime the Surfaces

Apply a high-quality bonding primer. This step is especially important if you are painting over laminate or previously stained cabinets. Use a foam roller for flat areas and an angled brush for corners and details.

Let the primer dry completely according to the manufacturer's instructions — usually 2 to 4 hours. Lightly sand with 220-grit paper and wipe clean before painting.

Step 5: Paint — Thin Coats Win the Race

This is where patience pays off. Apply your first coat of paint using a foam roller for large flat surfaces and a high-quality angled brush for edges and recessed panels. Do not overload your roller. Thin, even coats are the secret to a professional-looking finish.

Wait for the first coat to dry fully (check paint can for recommended dry time), then lightly sand with 220-grit paper, wipe clean, and apply a second coat. For most colors, two coats are enough. If you are painting white over dark cabinets, you may need three.

Step 6: Paint the Cabinet Frames

While the doors are drying, paint the cabinet frames using the same technique. Use a small foam roller for the interior panels and a brush for corners. Two thin coats here as well.

Step 7: Reassemble Carefully

Wait at least 24 hours after your final coat before reassembling. Paint needs time to cure fully. Install the hardware and hang the doors using your labeling system. Use a level to make sure everything is straight.

Pro Tips for Best Results

  • Choose an alkyd or enamel paint. Water-based acrylic paints labeled "cabinet and trim" also work well and clean up with soap and water.
  • Use painter's pyramids to dry painted doors — they lift the door off the surface so the edges do not stick or smudge.
  • Work in a well-ventilated area. Paint fumes can be strong even with low-VOC products.
  • Do not rush the drying time between coats. Rushing leads to peeling and visible brush marks.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the cleaning step. Grease will cause your paint to peel within months.
  • Using cheap brushes and rollers. Quality tools make a noticeable difference.
  • Painting with the cabinets still attached to the frame. You will end up with drips and a messy hinge line.
  • Applying thick coats. Thick coats take forever to dry and are prone to running and sagging.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What kind of paint should I use for kitchen cabinets? A: Look for an enamel or alkyd paint labeled specifically for cabinets and trim. Brands like Benjamin Moore Advance, Sherwin-Williams ProClassic, and Behr Alkyd Semi-Gloss are solid choices.

Q: Do I need to use a primer? A: Yes. Primer helps the paint adhere and prevents the old color from bleeding through. It is especially critical when painting over stained or dark cabinets.

Q: How long does the whole project take? A: Plan for three to four days. The actual painting is quick, but you need to account for drying and curing time between coats.

Q: Can I paint over laminate cabinets? A: Yes, but you need a bonding primer designed for laminate surfaces. Regular primer will peel off.

Final Thoughts

Painting your kitchen cabinets is one of those projects that looks intimidating on the surface but is entirely doable with the right preparation. The key is taking your time on the prep work — cleaning, sanding, and priming — and not rushing the painting process.
