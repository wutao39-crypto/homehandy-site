What to Look For in a Cordless Drill

Before jumping into the recommendations, here are the features that actually matter:

  • Voltage: 18V or 20V is the sweet spot for most DIY tasks. Higher voltage means more power but also more weight and cost.
  • Brushless motor: Brushless motors are more efficient, run cooler, and last longer than brushed motors. Well worth the extra cost.
  • Chuck size: A 1/2-inch chuck accepts the widest range of bits. Avoid 3/8-inch chucks.
  • Battery platform: Consider buying into a brand's battery system. If you already own other tools from a brand, sticking with the same battery platform saves money in the long run.
  • Weight: A drill that is too heavy will fatigue your wrist. Look for something under 4 pounds with the battery attached.
  • Clutch settings: More clutch settings give you finer control over screw driving depth. 15+ settings is ideal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is brushless worth the extra cost? A: Yes, for frequent use. Brushless motors are 30-50% more efficient, run cooler, and last significantly longer. For occasional use (once a month or less), a brushed motor is fine.

Q: Should I buy a drill kit or tool-only? A: If you are new to power tools, buy a kit that includes a battery and charger. If you already own other tools from the same brand, buying tool-only saves money.

Q: What voltage do I need? A: 18V or 20V (these are essentially the same) covers virtually all DIY tasks. 12V is fine for light work. Anything above 20V is overkill for home use.

Q: How long should a cordless drill last? A: A quality drill from a reputable brand should last 5-10 years with normal home use. Batteries typically need replacement after 2-4 years depending on usage.
